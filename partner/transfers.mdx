title: "Social Pay vs pocket transfer"
description: "Two different rails. Do not treat Social Pay as silent institutional treasury."
---

| Rail | How you call it | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Social payment** | Proforma `operation: social-payment` plus `destination.email`, `phone`, or `alias` → execute | Creates a Pay order. Funds can sit in **escrow** until claim, auto-claim, or expiry refund. Retail email UX applies. |
| **Pocket transfer** | Proforma with `destination.pocket` | Wallet movement between known pocket IDs. Does **not** go through the Pay escrow rail. |

## Social Pay

Execute with `POST /v1/wallet/transaction`. A successful execute returns a Pay order (`orderId`, `status: pending`, `expiryDate`, `walletMovementId`) — not an instant pocket credit.

List pending: `GET /v1/social-pay/order`.

Claim with a chosen pocket:

```http
POST /v1/social-pay/order/claim
```

Body is an array of objects with `orderId` and `pocketId`. Destination pocket **cannot** be selected at create — only at claim (or via auto-claim to the first suitable pocket).

WebSocket types: `social.payment.pending`, `social.payment.success`, `social.payment.expiration`.

## Pocket transfer

When both pocket UUIDs are known, `destination.pocket` is the closer API treasury move. Confirm end-to-end for parent ↔ subaccount in your environment before you promise both directions.

## What not to invent

- A unified “master-sub treasury product”
- A documented API flag to disable claim emails
- Auto-delivery guarantees for every company parent

If you need silent institutional moves, prefer pocket transfer and confirm with your integration manager.
